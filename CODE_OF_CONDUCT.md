# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

This project exists to help defenders do their work. We want contributing here to be
straightforward and free of hostility for everyone, regardless of experience level, employer,
background, disability, appearance, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, nationality,
personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith. Security people disagree about
  severity, detection logic, and risk all the time; argue the technical point, not the person.
- Be specific in review. "This regex backtracks on a 10 MB log line, here is a case" helps.
  "This is bad" does not.
- Respect that contributors work under different constraints: different tooling, different
  regulators, different budgets. A skill that does not fit your environment may fit someone else's.
- Credit others' work, and be honest about what you have and have not tested.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, commits, or anywhere else
  the project is discussed.
- Publishing others' private information, including workplace details or unredacted incident data.
- Posting real malware, working exploit code, live credentials, or another organization's
  confidential data. This is both a conduct issue and a security one; see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
- Sustained disruption, including reopening settled decisions to wear people down.

## Sharing incident data

Contributors often want to illustrate a point with something they saw in production. Sanitize
first: use `.example` domains, RFC 5737 and RFC 3849 address ranges, and invented hostnames and
user names. Never paste a real customer's data, a real employee's name, or an unredacted alert
into this repository. If you are unsure whether something is safe to share, it is not.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces (issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages) and
when representing the project publicly.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns by opening a GitHub issue if the matter is public and minor, or by
contacting the maintainer privately through their GitHub profile if it is not. Reports will be
reviewed and responded to as promptly as the maintainer is able. Maintainers who do not follow
or enforce this code in good faith may face consequences decided by the project's leadership.
